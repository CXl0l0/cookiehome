\chapter{Introduction}
\section{Background}
Even in this day and age of absurd technological advancements, user privacy preference remains an alien topic for the general public. There are several ways user privacy preferences can be collected online, some common ways that are used may be through surveys \cite{asif2022privacy}, device settings parsing \cite{noauthor_undated-vk}, and cookies' consent banner interaction \cite{Cao2025-gx}. This project will specifically be exploring how user privacy preferences are extracted through cookies, commonly presented to users with the interface of a popup provided by CMPs (Consent Management Platforms) called cookie banners \cite{noauthor_undated-lq} each time they visit a website.

Most people don’t really pay close attention to the user privacy preferences that they (have to) set each time they visit a new website on the Internet and is presented with a cookie banner. Whenever people are presented with a popup-like interface with verbose texts that they have to read through, the instinct for most people would be to get rid of the popup window. As such when combined with the assumption that the general public isn't sufficiently educated on the topic of the importance of user privacy preferences and how to manage them well, users tend to click the options presented on the interface that allows them to proceed immediately (i.e. usually the "Accept All" or "Deny All" options). Some reasons why people tend to ignore how user privacy preference extraction works in cookie banners is likely driven by the cookie banners' deliberately complex design and users' knowledge limitations \cite{Safna2025-ng}.

In view of this problem presented and potential causes that are established, this project aims to solve this issue by gamifying the process of user privacy preference extraction through cookie banners.

\section{Project Objectives}
Given the background of the project, the project aims to address the problems through primarily 3 objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Investigate existing solutions that attempts to address similar issues
  \item Design a solution that presents the management of cookies in a gamified interface
  \item Evaluate how a gamified process may be effective in helping users manage their privacy preferences within their own initiative
\end{enumerate}
With these 3 objectives as the foundational directive pointers for this report, the report will be detailing how the solution for this project came to ideation, the project's progress so far and it's subsequent analysis on how effective it is in achieving the objectives.
