Executive summary
Cookie Homes explores whether a gamified interface can help users understand cookie
purposes and express their privacy preferences. Websites become homes and cookies
become guests with requests. Users can inspect technical evidence, discuss a scenario with a
chatbot and review the resulting choice.
The current prototype combines a browser extension connector with a local website interface.
It demonstrates data collection, scenario generation and local decision updates. Its benefit to
users remains a research question; website consent write-back and a comparative user study
are planned.


At a glance
  • Motivation: improve the quality of cookie choices during everyday browsing.
  • Current scope: Cookiebot metadata, consent signals and public declarations.
  • Main question: does the gamified journey improve understanding and engagement while
    preserving users’ intended choices?


Motivation and problem
The report’s F1 and FIFA examples show users encountering consent controls before
continuing with a website. The controls differ in detail and granularity. These examples connect
the project to everyday browsing, but do not establish how all users behave or which interface
is best.
The problem is to make cookie requests understandable without distorting their meaning or
steering users towards acceptance or rejection. This is challenging because purposes can be
uncertain, preferences can depend on context, and conversational answers can be ambiguous.

Key findings
The reviewed literature supports studying consent interface design. It also provides an existing
approach to cookie classification. The proposed contribution is an evaluation of how the home
and guest experience supports users’ decisions.


Literature and background
Nouwens et al. [1] found that removing a first-page opt-out increased consent by 22–23
percentage points in a field experiment with 40 participants. CookieBlock [2] uses machine
learning to classify cookies and enforce preferences. These studies motivate attention to both
presentation and technical handling.


Key challenge
The reviewed works examine interface effects or automated classification and enforcement.
They do not evaluate this project’s conversational home and guest experience. Cookie Homes
therefore focuses on whether that experience helps people understand requests and express
preferences. This is a scoped research question, not a claim that all related approaches are
absent.



  Research question. Can a gamified scenario interface improve cookie understanding
  and engagement while accurately capturing users’ intended choices?




Implications
The analogy must stay connected to the available purpose evidence. Public declarations can
be incomplete or inaccurate, so using them does not remove uncertainty. The prototype should
make those limits visible.

  Challenge          Risk                              Design response

  Purpose evidence   Incomplete declarations           Show uncertainty and metadata

  Conversation       Ambiguous user intent             Clarify before changing a choice

Recommendations
Complete the supported Cookiebot journey, then evaluate user benefit. The following work
connects the implementation to measurable research outcomes.
  1. Complete the prototype. Move the interaction into the extension, investigate supported
     consent write-back, and add review and recovery controls. Preserve uncertain purpose
     labels and clarify ambiguous answers before changing state.

  2. Run a comparative study. Pilot matched tasks, then compare Cookie Homes with a
     category dashboard. Counterbalance order. Measure comprehension, completion rate,
     time, interpretation errors, decision confidence, engagement and perceived difficulty.
     Compare choices with stated intent and discuss mismatches with participants.

  3. Interpret the evidence. Use interviews to identify helpful or confusing elements. If
     feasible, compare a simplified variant with the same choice logic to separate the
     analogy’s effect from the chatbot’s. Otherwise report results for the whole interface.
     Agree sample size and analysis before recruitment.


Conclusion
The prototype demonstrates the feasibility of a scenario based interaction. The expected
outputs are a usable extension prototype, evidence about understanding and engagement, and
recommendations for presenting cookie choices.
Improvement is a hypothesis to test. Mixed or negative results will still inform the design. A
small exploratory study cannot establish broad effectiveness, and the current prototype does
not yet apply choices to a website’s CMP.

Appendix
Architecture and progress
The connector reads cookie metadata, the Cookiebot consent snapshot and a public
declaration. The adapter normalises inputs and matches purposes. Cookie Homes turns the
resulting records into scenarios, with the underlying evidence available for inspection.
Chat messages pass through the local server to Gemini for interpretation. The rules engine
validates structured decisions and updates local state. Essential guests are protected. Unclear
messages should trigger clarification; consent write-back is shown as planned.
